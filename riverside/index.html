How Riverside Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of the year.

The earlier you report a comfort problem, the cheaper it usually is. A weak capacitor costs little to replace, yet the same part, ignored through a full season, can take out the compressor and turn a simple fix into a full system replacement.
